Amoebas of the same size merge when they touch — they fuse into one amoeba a single cell bigger. Shape doesn't matter, only size.

The fused blob has extra cells, and they melt from the top: highest first, leftmost within a row, skipping any cell whose loss would split the amoeba. The survivor keeps the lower part of the fused shape.

Merging is the only way to score. Fusing three or more at once scores extra, but the result still grows just one size.

Merges can cascade: the fused blob shrinks, everything settles, and new touches can merge again.

The lit numbers above the dish show which sizes are dropping; the mix shifts as your level rises. A red number is a rescue — that size is still on the board, so it stays in the draw.

Sizes 10 and 11 never drop — you can only grow them. When two size-11 elderberry amoebas meet, the size-12 amoeba they fuse into breaks the bounds of the dish — the first of its kind to leave home — and settles the colony.

The colony is beyond your reach and beyond all danger. There is no up or down out there: settlers drift toward their common center of mass, merge on contact, and pack into a little round world. Past the elderberry the berries cycle around — size 12 is a white currant again, born under a new sun.
